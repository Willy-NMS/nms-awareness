Think before you click

  • Phishing emails pretend to come from someone you trust to steal passwords, money or data.
  • Watch for urgency, threats, prizes or anything you were not expecting.
  • When in doubt, don't click. Call the sender at a number you already know.

Phishing is the most common way attackers get into a business. The message looks like it comes from Microsoft, a bank, a shipping company, a vendor or even a coworker.

Red flags: a sense of urgency ("your account will be closed today"), a request for a password or payment, a link that does not match the company, bad grammar, or an attachment you did not expect.

If something feels off, stop. Do not click links or open attachments. Contact the person or company using a phone number or website you already trust, or call NMS.
